# findmyrun.club — Strategy & Next Steps

## The Core Insight

findmyrun fills a real gap: there's no single directory for casual, social, and non-affiliated running clubs in the UK. England Athletics and RunTogether only cover affiliated clubs. The informal groups — social runs, run/coffee clubs, influencer-led crews, pub runs — don't appear anywhere centralised. That's the lane to own.

## Validation So Far

- Reddit post: 13,000 views — people care about this problem
- 242 page views from a single post — decent conversion for Reddit
- Users tried to search but couldn't find their area — the concept works, data is the bottleneck
- Multiple comments along the lines of "I would use this"
- No one said "this already exists" or "I don't need this"

---

## Phase 1: Win Leicester (Weeks 1-2)

**Goal:** Make findmyrun the most complete directory of running clubs in Leicester.

**Why Leicester first:**
- Home city — you can physically verify clubs, attend runs, build relationships
- Smaller city = achievable to be genuinely comprehensive in 1-2 weeks
- Proves the model and gives you a template for expanding
- A Leicester runner who finds every single club listed will tell their mates

**Target:** 25-30 clubs covering the full range (social, affiliated, trail, track, women's, beginners)

**How to find clubs:**
- Instagram hashtags: #leicesterrunclub #leicesterrunning #leicesterrunners
- Facebook: search "Leicester running club" and "Leicester runners" groups
- RunTogether: https://groups.runtogether.co.uk (search Leicester area)
- Strava: search running clubs by location
- parkrun: check Leicestershire parkrun social pages — runners there know every local club
- Google: "run club Leicester" "running group Leicester" "social run Leicester"
- Word of mouth: DM organisers you find — ask who else runs locally
- Local press/blogs: Leicester Mercury, etc.

**When adding clubs:**
- Use the spreadsheet template provided
- Be specific with meeting points (not just "city centre" — exact location)
- Get Instagram handles where possible — lets people verify the club is real and active
- For lat/lng: right-click on Google Maps → "What's here?" → copy coordinates

## Phase 4: SEO — The Long Game (Ongoing from Week 1)

**Why this matters:** Directory services grow through search, not social posts. Someone googles "run clubs in Leicester" and finds you. That's the primary growth channel.

**Actions:**
- **Individual club pages:** Each club should have its own URL (/club/manchester-road-runners) rather than just a modal. Each page is a separate indexable result that could rank.
- **Richer city pages:** Add genuine editorial content per city — not AI filler, but real descriptions of the running scene. Mention landmarks, popular routes, the vibe.
- **Structured data markup:** Add LocalBusiness or SportsActivityLocation schema so Google shows rich results.
- **Long-tail keywords:** Target searches like "beginner run club Leicester", "women's running group Manchester", "trail running club Leeds"
- **Blog/content (optional):** Short posts like "Best run clubs in Leicester for beginners" that link to your listings. These rank well and drive traffic.

---

## Phase 5: Make Club Organisers Your Growth Engine

**The claiming system is strategically important.** A club organiser who claims their listing:
- Keeps their own data updated (solves the maintenance problem)
- Shares their listing with members (free distribution)
- Tells other organisers about it (network effect)

**Actions:**
- Make "Claim this club" more prominent on listings
- Reach out directly to organisers when you add their club — "Hey, we've listed [club name] on findmyrun.club — want to claim it and manage your details?"
- The "I'm coming" attendance feature gives organisers a genuine reason to care — it's a headcount tool they can't get elsewhere

---

## Phase 6: Sustainability & The "Used Once" Problem

The Reddit comment about "can't imagine people using it more than once" is partially valid. But:

- **Being used once is fine** — if you help someone find their club, that's the job done. Google Maps doesn't worry about people using it once to find a restaurant.
- **The real returning users are club organisers** — they manage listings, check attendance
- **Organic submissions solve the maintenance problem** — once you have enough traffic, clubs add themselves

**Optional features for return visits (later, not now):**
- "New clubs near you" email alerts
- Seasonal content: "winter running clubs", "couch to 5k groups starting in January"
- Event listings (you've already built this — activate it when you have enough clubs)

---

## What NOT to Do Right Now

- **Don't build more features.** The app has enough functionality. Map view, filters, attendance, claiming, submissions — it's all there. Every hour coding is an hour not adding clubs.
- **Don't try to cover all of the UK at once.** Thin coverage everywhere is worse than deep coverage in 3 cities.
- **Don't compete with EA/RunTogether head-on.** Your lane is the informal clubs they don't cover.
- **Don't worry about monetisation yet.** Traffic comes first. Revenue options later include: featured listings, gear brand sponsorship, race event promotion partnerships.
- **Don't post on Reddit again until you have much better data coverage.** You get one chance at a first impression in each subreddit. Make it count.
